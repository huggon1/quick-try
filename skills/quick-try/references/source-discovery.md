# Source and social discovery

Read when current user practice, examples, failure reports, prompts, or community reactions could materially shape the trial. This branch supports a specific experience question; it is not a general social-listening or feed-collection workflow.

## Discover available coverage

Inspect the current host's available skills, connectors, MCP tools, Web search, and authenticated services before choosing sources. Follow the instructions of a selected social-research capability. Do not infer access from a tool's brand or install/configure a missing integration during read-only reconnaissance.

Classify coverage precisely:

- **Platform-aware:** the available tool searches or reads the named platform with known scope.
- **Public Web:** a search engine may surface public posts but cannot establish complete platform coverage, private content, live engagement, or feed ranking.
- **User-provided:** links or files supplied by the user; inspect their actual content and date.
- **Unavailable or unknown:** report the gap and whether it affects sample design or interpretation.

If useful coverage is unavailable, propose a user-provided link, a later run on another capable host, or a narrower first-party-only scene. Do not replace missing social evidence with invented consensus.

## Collect for the trial

Start with current first-party documentation for identity, supported setup, inputs, limits, and product claims. Select community or social items only when they contribute one of these:

- an actual input and output worth reproducing;
- a failure mode or setup obstacle to include;
- a meaningful variation the user could try;
- a disputed claim the trial can test;
- a usage pattern that changes the prepared scene.

Try each relevant available platform that could contain real use, setup, or failure reports. Do not stop at the first convenient source when another available platform may change the scene. Preserve the source URL, platform, author when available, publication date, access date, relevant conditions, and why it affects this trial. Treat engagement counts and ranking as time-bound observations. Keep quoted material within applicable limits and avoid redistributing assets without permission.

Stop when additional items no longer change the trial design. Use every useful signal found and do not fill a quota with weak mentions. In the user-facing report, explain the report, its source, and what it changes in the proposed trial. Keep source-health details and search mechanics in an attached record unless a missing platform materially narrows the result. Social posts inform expectations and variations; only this run's executed evidence becomes an observation.

Broad monitoring, scheduled collection, audience analysis, trend reporting, and maintaining a feed are separate workflows. Hand a selected signal into Quick Try when the user wants to operate something and test a concrete question.
